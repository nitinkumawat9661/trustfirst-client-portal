# Domain Model

## Overview

The domain model is centered on tenants that manage many clients, and clients that may have many engagements across different service categories. A project is one possible engagement structure, not the entire domain.

## Primary Concepts

### Tenant

A subscribing business using TrustFirst Client Portal. A tenant may be an agency, consultancy, studio, systems integrator, marketing firm, AI practice, ERP implementer, or managed services provider.

### Client Organization

An external customer served by a tenant. A client organization can have contacts, files, requirements, engagements, approvals, invoices, and lifecycle status.

### Client Contact

A person associated with a client organization. Contacts can have different access levels across engagements.

### Engagement

A commercial unit of work for a client. Examples include a branding package, AI readiness audit, website redesign, ERP rollout, marketing campaign, or support retainer.

### Project

A structured delivery container inside an engagement when the work requires milestones, timelines, deliverables, tasks, or approvals.

### Service Line

A tenant-defined category of work, such as Branding, AI Automation, Website, ERP, Marketing, Software, or Consulting.

### Requirement

A structured statement of client needs, constraints, preferences, assets, expectations, or acceptance criteria.

### Deliverable

An output produced for a client, such as a brand guide, website, campaign report, AI workflow, ERP configuration, content calendar, prototype, strategy document, or software release.

### Approval

A formal decision point where an authorized person accepts, rejects, or requests changes.

### File Asset

A stored document, media asset, contract, source file, brand asset, dataset, export, deliverable, or evidence artifact.

### Notification

A message generated by portal activity, schedule rules, approvals, reminders, or tenant-configured events.

### Audit Event

An immutable record of important user, system, permission, lifecycle, file, or approval activity.

## Domain Boundaries

### Identity and Access

Users, roles, permissions, tenant membership, client contact access, and invitations.

### Client Management

Client organizations, contacts, lifecycle status, account ownership, and relationship history.

### Engagement Management

Service lines, engagement types, projects, deliverables, milestones, requirements, and status.

### Collaboration

Comments, approvals, notifications, files, review requests, and client-facing activity.

### Operations

Tenant settings, templates, audit logs, usage, billing metadata, and integrations.

## Domain Rule Summary

- A tenant owns its client organizations.
- A client organization may have many engagements.
- An engagement may or may not contain a project.
- A project may contain milestones, tasks, deliverables, files, approvals, and requirements.
- Requirements can exist at client, engagement, or project scope.
- Files must always have ownership, scope, visibility, and audit metadata.
- Permissions must be evaluated by tenant, role, scope, and object ownership.
